# Financials: Cost, Revenue, Pricing & Steam Economics

## The Reality Check

Before any numbers, the sobering baseline:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Median indie game lifetime revenue on Steam | ~$4,000 |
| Median indie game revenue in 2024 | ~$180 |
| % of indie devs that are solo | 55% |
| Solo dev failure rate | 70% |
| % of Steam devs who have made >$100k gross | 10% |
| % of indie games making >$100k | 15% |
| % of indie devs who have made >$1M | 3% (~1,500 developers total) |
| % of indie games released in 2024 that are financially viable | ~0.5% |
| Top 1% share of total indie revenue | 90% |

The indie game market is a power law distribution. The vast majority earn almost nothing. The tiny minority earn fortunes. Understanding where the threshold is — and what pushes games across it — is the point of this research.

## Development Cost Ranges

### Solo Developer Budget Tiers

| Tier | Budget | Description | Example |
|------|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Zero Budget** | $0-$1K | Self-funded, free tools, existing hardware | Vampire Survivors (initially), Buckshot Roulette |
| **Shoestring** | $1K-$10K | Some asset purchases, maybe freelance art/music | Many game jam expansions |
| **Modest** | $10K-$50K | Dedicated development period, some outsourcing | Undertale ($50K Kickstarter), Hollow Knight (AU$57K) |
| **Serious** | $50K-$200K | Full-time development for 1-2 years, professional assets | Many mid-tier Steam releases |
| **Ambitious** | $200K+ | Multi-year, publisher-backed, contracted work | Manor Lords (Patreon + Epic MegaGrant) |

### What the Money Actually Goes To

For a typical solo-dev project:
- **$0:** Engine (Unity free <$200K revenue, Godot free, Unreal free <$1M)
- **$0-$20:** Art tools (Aseprite $20, Blender free, GIMP free)
- **$100-$2,000:** Asset store purchases (sprites, SFX, music)
- **$0-$5,000:** Freelance art/music (if not doing everything yourself)
- **$100:** Steam Direct fee (one-time per game)
- **$1K-$10K:** Legal/IP filing (optional but recommended if you have a hit)
- **$2K-$10K:** Hardware/testing equipment
- **$0-$5,000:** Marketing (most successful solo devs spent $0 on paid marketing)

### Hidden Costs Most Devs Overlook
- **Platform fees:** Steam takes 30% (drops to 25% after $10M, 20% after $50M)
- **Post-launch patches:** Budget 10-20% extra for bug fixes and updates
- **Taxes:** Varies by country, but can take 20-40% of remaining revenue
- **Opportunity cost:** Years of full-time work without salary

### Actual Developer Take-Home
On average, a self-publishing indie developer sees about **25% of gross revenue** after:
- Steam's 30% cut
- Government taxes
- Refunds (~5-10% of sales)
- Other fees and costs

So a game that grosses $100,000 on Steam might net the developer ~$25,000 before personal expenses.

### Real-World Budget Breakdown: A First-Time Solo Dev

One developer documented their first Steam launch:
- 410 copies sold in month 1
- $632 in revenue
- After Steam cut, taxes, and development costs: **net negative**
- Conclusion: "In terms of cash, it was not worth it"

This is the typical experience, not the exception.

## Pricing Strategy

### The Data on What Works

| Price Point | Perception | Best For | Examples |
|------------|------------|----------|----------|
| **$2.99-$4.99** | "Fuck it, why not" impulse buy | Ultra-short games, game jam expansions | Buckshot Roulette ($5), Vampire Survivors ($5) |
| **$7.99** | **The sweet spot** — feels like $5, is actually $8 | Short-medium indie games, co-op | Content Warning ($8), Peak ($5) |
| **$9.99-$14.99** | Considered but still accessible | Medium-length quality games | Lethal Company ($10), Undertale ($10), Balatro ($15), Stardew Valley ($15), Hollow Knight ($15) |
| **$15-$19.99** | Premium indie | Content-rich, polished experiences | Upper range of solo dev viability |
| **$20-$29.99** | Ceiling for most indies | Ambitious projects with substantial content | "Charging >$25 is getting trickier" |
| **$30+** | Rare for solo devs | Only with exceptional scope or brand | Manor Lords ($40) — atypical |

### Key Pricing Insights

**The $7.99 Psychology:**
The developers of *Peak* and *Content Warning* chose $7.99 because: $12 feels like $10, but $13 starts feeling like $15. $7.99 feels closer to $5 despite being closer to $10. This creates the largest gap between perceived cost and actual price. Content Warning sold 5M+ copies at this price.

**The Median is Dropping:**
The median price of best-selling new Steam games dropped 20% from 2023-2025. The mean barely changed (down 2%), meaning the top end held steady but more hits are coming from the $5-15 range.

**The $20 Ceiling:**
Small developers have settled on a ~$20 price limit. Above $25, players increasingly compare value to the deep catalog of $10-15 indie titles available. There's a "dead zone" between $20 and $60 where few games of any size live.

**Cheaper Games Convert Better:**
Wishlist-to-sales conversion for games >$10 at launch: median 0.10x.
For all games with >10K wishlists: median 0.17x.
Cheaper games have notably higher conversion rates.

**Launch Discounts Don't Help Much:**
"When you release your game, people who are interested would buy it no matter if it's full price or 50% off. The price isn't the main factor for them."

## Steam Revenue Splits & Economics

### The Fee Structure
| Revenue Tier | Steam's Cut | Dev's Share |
|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| First $10M | 30% | 70% |
| $10M - $50M | 25% | 75% |
| $50M+ | 20% | 80% |

### Publisher Involvement
If you use a publisher (e.g., Hooded Horse for Manor Lords, Chucklefish for early Stardew Valley):
- Publisher typically takes 20-50% of the developer's share
- In exchange: funding, marketing, QA, porting, bizdev
- ConcernedApe estimates he gets 50-60% of gross sales after publisher and distribution splits

### Self-Publishing Math (Solo Dev)

For a $15 game selling 10,000 copies:
```
Gross Revenue:           $150,000
Steam Cut (30%):        -$45,000
Refunds (~7%):          -$10,500
Remaining:               $94,500
Income Tax (~25%):      -$23,625
Net to Developer:        $70,875
```

For the median indie game ($4,000 gross):
```
Gross Revenue:            $4,000
Steam Cut (30%):         -$1,200
Refunds (~7%):             -$280
Remaining:                $2,520
Income Tax (~25%):         -$630
Net to Developer:         $1,890
```

## Financial Planning for Solo Devs

### Minimum Viable Financial Plan
1. **Assume your game will make <$10K.** Plan your life around that being the baseline outcome.
2. **Keep your day job** or have savings to cover 1-2 years of development.
3. **Ship your first game quickly** (3-6 months). Don't spend 4 years on game #1.
4. **Each subsequent game sells ~40% better** on average. Build a catalog.
5. **Target $0 marketing budget.** Focus on streamers, Steam Next Fest, and social media.
6. **Price between $5-15.** Don't overthink it.
7. **If you get a hit, the money will be life-changing.** If you don't, you'll have the skills for game #2.

### The "Portfolio Career" Model
The developers who sustain themselves long-term tend to:
- Release 3-5 smaller games over several years
- Build an audience incrementally
- Each game funds the development of the next
- Eventually one hits — and the back catalog also starts selling
