# Marketing & Discovery: How Solo Indie Games Get Found

## The Landscape

Over 18,000 games launched on Steam in 2024. Only ~232 games in H1 2024 received 1,000+ reviews (the rough threshold for "hit" status). If your game has under 50 reviews, **Steam's algorithm essentially ignores you.**

The good news: most successful solo-dev games spent $0 on paid marketing. The bad news: they all did *something* right to get discovered.

---

## How Steam's Algorithm Actually Works

### Pre-Launch: Wishlists
- Wishlists affect visibility **only before launch** via the "Popular Upcoming" section
- Valve has stated: "By and large, wishlists don't really have an effect on your visibility on Steam"
- However, wishlists generate direct email notifications on launch and sales — this is their real value
- The widely-cited "30,000 wishlists" target exists because it increases odds of hitting revenue thresholds for algorithmic promotion post-launch

### Post-Launch: Revenue is King
- Once your game launches, **Steam's algorithm prioritizes revenue generation**
- The "New & Trending" section is driven by sales velocity, not wishlist count
- A game can have 200 wishlists and still blow up if it generates rapid sales (e.g., Metal Garden: ~200 wishlists at launch, ~38,000 sales via influencer-driven purchases)

### The Virtuous Cycle
External traffic spike (streamer, TikTok, festival) -> Sales surge -> Steam algorithm notices -> "New & Trending" placement -> Organic Steam traffic -> More sales -> Steam promotes more

**The key insight:** You need to generate a concentrated spike of external traffic. Steady trickles don't trigger the algorithm. You need *bursts*.

## The Five Marketing Channels That Actually Work

### 1. Content Creators / Streamers (Highest Impact)

**The single most effective marketing channel for indie games.**

Conversion rates:
- **Twitch:** 30-40% conversion to wishlists per average viewer (1,000 avg viewers = 300-400 wishlists)
- **YouTube:** 3-10% conversion rate
- **Combined effect:** A single well-placed streamer can generate thousands of sales in hours

**How to approach streamers:**
- Research creators who play games similar to yours
- Focus on **micro-influencers** in your niche (500-10K followers) — they have higher engagement rates and are more likely to respond
- Send personalized pitches (not mass emails)
- Provide free keys well before launch
- Time outreach with Steam Next Fest or launch

**Case Study — The Ouroboros King:**
- Chess-based roguelike struggled with only 500 wishlists in 10 months
- Developer pivoted to targeted streamer outreach
- Personalized pitches timed with Steam Next Fest
- Result: 500 -> 20,000 wishlists in two weeks

**Case Study — Metal Garden:**
- ~200 wishlists at launch
- Developer actively reached out to gaming YouTubers
- Influencer coverage drove ~38,000 estimated sales
- Proves you don't need wishlists if you have streamers

**Case Study — Lethal Company:**
- Zeekerss had built a small audience through 19 previous games
- Used existing followers for pre-release playtesting
- Streamers picked it up organically because the game is inherently entertaining to watch
- Snowballed into 100K concurrent players

### 2. TikTok (Rising Fast)

**One of the most powerful platforms for indie game virality in 2024-2025.**

**Case Study — Nubby's Number Factory:**
- Only ~1,500 wishlists at launch
- Sold estimated 200,000 copies in the first month
- Strategy: short, high-energy video clips designed to immediately capture attention
- "Behind the scenes" dev videos also went viral (one tutorial on retro 3D graphics: 1.5M views)

**Case Study — Another Crab's Treasure:**
- 360,000 TikTok followers, 10M likes
- Close developer-marketing collaboration
- "Investing dev time into social media and content creation can make a huge difference"

**What works on TikTok:**
- Visually striking gameplay clips (the first 1-2 seconds must hook)
- "How I made this" development process videos
- Dramatic/funny moments from the game
- Before/after comparisons (early prototype vs. final)
- Frequency matters — post consistently

### 3. Steam Next Fest (Structural Advantage)

**The single best free marketing opportunity on Steam.**

How it works:
- Free event where you demo your game alongside thousands of others
- For the first 2 days, Steam distributes visibility more evenly
- After day 2, a machine learning algorithm takes over and promotes games getting the most engagement

**How to break through during Next Fest:**
Games that reach the "magic tier" typically have one of:
- A viral social post (usually TikTok)
- A wave of content creators streaming the demo
- A major showcase timed before Next Fest
- A coordinated plan: demo updates + press + content creator engagement + new trailer

**Timing your Next Fest:**
- Submit to the LAST Next Fest before your actual launch
- Don't demo too early — wishlist decay is real if launch is far away

### 4. Reddit / Twitter (X) / Discord (Community Building)

**Organic community, not paid promotion.**

Reddit can generate massive spikes:
- r/gaming, r/indiegaming, r/steam — a front-page post can generate thousands of wishlists overnight
- But you can't force virality on Reddit. The game needs a visually compelling hook.

Twitter/X is useful for:
- Building developer following over time
- Connecting with other devs (cross-promotion)
- GIF posts of interesting mechanics/art
- "Devlog" threads showing progress

Discord is essential for:
- Beta testing community
- Post-launch support and feedback
- Building word-of-mouth (dedicated fans recruit friends)
- Modding community support

### 5. Festivals & Showcases (Beyond Steam Next Fest)

- Submit your demo to every online festival that applies
- Day of the Devs, PAX Online, LudoNarraCon, Tiny Teams Festival, etc.
- Each festival generates a small spike of wishlists
- Cumulative effect: steady wishlist growth between major events

## Wishlist-to-Sales Conversion Data

### 2024-2025 Benchmarks

| Pre-Launch Wishlists | Median Week 1 Conversion | Expected Week 1 Sales |
|---------------------|--------------------------|----------------------|
| 5,000 | ~0.17x | ~850 |
| 10,000 | ~0.17x | ~1,700 |
| 25,000 | ~0.15x | ~3,750 |
| 50,000 | ~0.15x | ~7,500 |
| 100,000 | ~0.15x | ~15,000 |

**Important caveats:**
- Variance is enormous: actual conversion can be 10x lower or 20x higher than median
- Cheaper games convert significantly better
- Games priced >$10: median drops to 0.10x
- Early Access games convert lower (~0.20x of standard launches)
- First-month total sales are roughly 27% of launch wishlists

### What Hurts Conversion

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Poor reviews** (below 80%) | Underperformers had median 67% positive reviews vs. 91% for hits |
| **Too long in pre-release** | Underperformers averaged 411 days pre-release vs. 214 for overperformers |
| **Sequel fatigue** | Sequels tend to convert lower |
| **"Boutique indie" curiosity** | Unique games rack up wishlists from curious people who never buy |
| **Launch discounts** | Minimal impact — interested buyers purchase at any price |

### What Helps Conversion

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Very Positive or higher reviews** | 91%+ positive reviews correlate strongly with high conversion |
| **Social/co-op elements** | Multiplayer games have built-in network effects |
| **Streamer coverage at launch** | Concentrated traffic spikes trigger Steam's algorithm |
| **Price under $10** | Higher impulse purchase rate |
| **Strong demo (via Next Fest)** | Converts demo players into wishlisters who convert to buyers |

## The Solo Dev Marketing Playbook

### Phase 1: Pre-Development (Before You Build)
1. Research what's selling on Steam RIGHT NOW
2. Ensure your concept has a clear, visual hook
3. Ask: "Would this make a good 10-second TikTok clip?"

### Phase 2: During Development (6-18 months before launch)
1. Post development updates on Twitter/TikTok (weekly)
2. Build a Discord server for interested players
3. Create your Steam store page as early as possible (start collecting wishlists)
4. Share GIFs and short clips of interesting mechanics

### Phase 3: Pre-Launch (3-6 months before)
1. Build a playable demo
2. Submit to Steam Next Fest (the last one before launch)
3. Submit demo to every relevant festival
4. Start sending keys to micro-influencers and streamers
5. Run a Steam Playtest for early feedback

### Phase 4: Launch Week
1. Coordinate streamer coverage for launch day
2. Post launch trailer on all social channels
3. Engage actively on Reddit, Discord, Steam forums
4. Respond to every Steam review (signals active development)
5. Monitor and fix critical bugs immediately

### Phase 5: Post-Launch
1. Regular updates (weekly in early access, monthly for 1.0)
2. Participate in Steam seasonal sales
3. Continue streamer outreach
4. Apply for Steam Daily Deal (significant visibility boost)
5. Consider major content updates as "re-launch" moments

---

## What NOT to Do

- **Don't spend money on paid ads** (they have low conversion and poor wishlist quality for indie games)
- **Don't tweet into the void** without visual content (text posts don't drive wishlists)
- **Don't announce too early** (411-day average pre-release period correlates with underperformance)
- **Don't ignore Steam Next Fest** (it's the single best free marketing opportunity)
- **Don't mass-email streamers** with generic pitches (personalize, target, be specific)
- **Don't expect your game to "speak for itself"** (even great games need initial exposure)

---

## The Uncomfortable Math

- You will not get to 100,000 wishlists by tweeting every day
- True wishlist growth comes from **concentrated spikes of external traffic**
- A developer can earn ~150 reviews through their own marketing effort
- To get to 1,000+ reviews, **the Steam playerbase must genuinely like your game**
- The algorithm picks up on genuine player enthusiasm and promotes it
- You can get players to your page, but only the GAME can convert them

**Bottom line:** Marketing gets people to look at your game. Only the game itself can make them buy and recommend it.
